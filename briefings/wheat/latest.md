KC HRW cash slipped to $7.05/bu, down 1.5% on the week from $7.17. That's a modest move given trailing vol has been running around 1.7% weekly, so nothing outside the recent range. Wheat was the weak leg in grains this week while corn and soybeans both firmed, consistent with the broader wrap headline — suggests the pressure is more wheat-specific than a risk-off move across the complex.

National export sales (all wheat classes, week ending Nov 12) came in at 344,799 tonnes, about 47,500 tonnes below the trailing 8-week average of 392,318. Pace is softening but not collapsing — still a solid absolute number, just below its recent run rate. Worth noting this data lags the price week by about a week and covers all wheat classes, not just KC HRW, so don't over-read a direct link to the cash price move.

On the headline side, the Baltic Sea attack on Russian export routes could, in theory, support a risk premium in global wheat, and Egypt's shift toward Poland hints at buyers diversifying away from traditional suppliers — but neither shows up clearly in this week's softer price or sales pace, so treat as background context only.

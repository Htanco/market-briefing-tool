KC HRW cash bid eased to $7.18/bu, down 1.5% on the week, continuing a soft tone that took wheat to its lowest level since January by Friday. Headlines this week were mixed and partly offsetting - rallies tied to Black Sea strikes early in the week gave way to broader weakness as talk of a trilateral deal potentially reopening Black Sea export flows weighed on the complex. Net effect was price giving back the prior week's gain. Given the choppy, conflicting headline flow, vol stayed contained at 1.76% (trailing 8-week stdev), so the move looks orderly rather than panicky.

On export sales (national, all wheat classes, week ending 11/12): net sales came in at 344.8k tons, about 47.5k below the trailing 8-week average pace of 392.3k. That's a modest slowdown in new sales activity, not a dramatic drop-off, and it's a different wheat stream than the KC cash price, so don't read this as HRW-specific demand weakness.

Watch: any confirmation or breakdown in the Black Sea trilateral talks next week, since that's the swing factor for whether this week's price softness extends or reverses.

Henry Hub dropped sharply this week, settling at $2.65/MMBtu, down 12.25% ($0.37) from $3.02. That's a big weekly move relative to the recent trading range — the 8-week vol measure sits at 5.87%, so this week's decline is roughly double the typical weekly swing.

Inventory data points the other way on the surprise front: storage built by 80 Bcf, well above the trailing 8-week average build of 57.5 Bcf, a surprise of +22.5 Bcf. A larger-than-expected injection with storage at 3,724 Bcf supports the case for downward price pressure heading into the shoulder season, and lines up directionally with the sell-off, though the size of the price move looks outsized relative to the storage surprise alone.

Headlines this week are mostly noise for pricing purposes — LNG export commentary, a Colorado ballot measure, and a BC infrastructure cost dispute don't give a clean read on the US supply/demand balance. The one WSJ note on futures rising for a second session suggests some intraweek bounce-back, but the net weekly print is still firmly negative.

Watch item: next week's storage report, to see if injections continue running above seasonal average into early November, which would reinforce bearish pressure into winter positioning.

WTI closed the week at $96.88, down $1.19 (-1.2%) from $98.07. That's a modest pullback despite a draw in crude stocks that came in well above trend — inventories fell 3.06mm bbls vs. an 8-week average draw of ~1.65mm, a surprise of -1.4mm bbls. Normally a bigger-than-expected draw would support price, so the decline here suggests the move was driven more by headline/geopolitical risk repricing than fundamentals this week.

Headlines are mixed and somewhat contradictory: reports of a US State Department warning briefly boosted prices intraday, while separately Persian Gulf exports are reportedly back above pre-war levels, easing supply-disruption fears even as attacks (drone intercepted near a Saudi field) continue. Net effect looks like the market is discounting the attack risk given exports are flowing normally, which likely capped upside despite the bullish inventory data. Vol remains elevated at 6.18% (8-week stdev of weekly returns), consistent with headline-driven two-way swings rather than a clean trend.

Watch: whether Gulf export flows hold up through next week's data — any disruption despite current "flowing like before" narrative would be a sharp re-pricing catalyst.

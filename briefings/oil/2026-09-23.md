WTI closed the week at $103.54, up $4.46 (+4.5%) from $99.08. That's a big weekly move, and it's consistent with the trailing 8-week vol reading of ~6% - we're clearly in a more volatile stretch than usual, not a one-off spike.

Inventories actually built, up 2,969 kb versus an 8-week average build of ~1,469 kb - a positive surprise of about 1,500 kb. Normally a build that size would cap prices, so the fact crude still rallied hard says the price action is being driven by something other than the domestic balance sheet this week.

Headlines point to broader macro pressure (equities down on rising yields alongside oil) and supply-side noise out of Venezuela - imports to the US reportedly hit a 9-year high, but freight costs are snarling spot sales, which could be tightening effective availability even as headline volumes rise. The heating oil release request from senators also signals political sensitivity to the price surge, though no action has been announced.

Watch item: any White House response on emergency heating oil releases, given bipartisan pressure and the run-up in prices.
